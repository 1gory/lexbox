A Year Abroad: How I Finally Learned to Think in English

By Maria Keller · 6 min read

When I first arrived in Dublin, I was reluctant to speak. My grammar was fine on paper, but every conversation felt like a test. For the first few weeks I had to put up with blank stares at the café and long silences on the phone.

Things changed when I started wandering through the city with a small notebook. I wrote down every phrase I could not figure out, and in the evening I wandered through my notes again, looking for patterns. Soon I noticed that I remembered a word far better when I could see the sentence where I had met it.

By spring I was no longer translating in my head. I still came across plenty of unfamiliar words, but now I could usually guess their meaning, and the ones that mattered gradually became part of my everyday vocabulary. Becoming fluent, I realised, is less about talent than about patience.
